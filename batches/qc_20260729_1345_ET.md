## §3 Accuracy spot-check

**30 cells across 12 terminals in 7 countries**, run by three research subagents (Italy / Aegean / West).

**Read the sample selection carefully before reading the verdicts.** The sample was drawn *specifically from cells where two independent research passes disagree* — Natalia's applied live values versus the never-applied `sw-europe` agentic draft of 2026-07-17. It is deliberately adversarial, not a random stratified draw. So the raw "live DB wrong on 20 of 30" figure is **conditional on a known disagreement** and must not be read as a 67% error rate on the dataset. The right reading is: *when the two passes disagreed, the draft was more often right.*

Verdicts: `we_staged_correct` 17 · `db_now_correct` 9 · `neither_correct` 3 · `unsupported` 1.
By the SOP's own §6 metric — cells where the cited ref does not support the current value — the count is **1 of 30 (3.3%)**, well under the 10% systemic flag. No escalation.

### Cells where the live database is wrong — ranked

**1. Italy · Taranto LNG Terminal `G100001095559` — `Status` = `operating`, `ActualStartYear` = 2027. Fix first.**
This is the 12 bcm/y Molo Polisettoriale project (95% Denali GNL / 5% Belenergia — matches the `Owner` field), not the small bunkering facility. The Postgres timeline is internally incoherent: a `proposed`/Oct-2025 entry followed by an `operating`/*actual*/**2027** entry — a future year. Its own `Status [ref]` does not contain any operating claim and is actually about the VIA procedure opening. Multiple Feb–Jul 2026 Italian sources confirm the environmental assessment was suspended over a port-concession conflict, reopened, and ran public consultation through July 2026. Construction has not started.
→ `Status` = **proposed**; move **2027** out of `ActualStartYear` into `LatestPlannedStartYear` (currently blank). *I verified this incoherence directly against the export rather than taking it on the subagent's word.*

**2. Italy · Portovesme FSRU `G100002105800` — `ShelvedYear`/`StopYear` = 2025, contradicted by GEM's own citation.**
The cited ref is the July-2023 LNG Prime piece reporting that Snam's notice-to-proceed deadline for the *Golar Arctic* conversion lapsed — the real dated shelving event. **I ran the cited URL through `url_verifier.py` myself: it PASSES on token "2023" and FAILS on "2025".** 2025 looks like an undated "no news in 2 years" inference. Snam's Mar-2026 Strategic Plan omits Portovesme entirely, consistent with 2023.
→ both years **2023**.

**3. Italy · Porto Empedocle `G100002047800` — `shelved`/2025, but the project is actively progressing.**
The timeline flags this as "inferred 2 y — last mentioned November 2023", yet Sicily's TAR rejected environmentalists' appeal (Jun 2025), MASE technical documents were filed (Jan 2026), and Snam's Dec-2025 Ten-Year Plan lists it as an active third-party project with the construction deadline extended to April 2028 — **matching GEM's own `LatestPlannedStartYear` = 2028**. ## §4 Post-apply check

**This pass is off-label and its numbers must be read with that caveat.** §3.4 assumes a batch the user has *applied*; the only prior batch covering this scope — `lng_terminals_batch_20260717_1246_ET_sw-europe_exhaustive_update.xlsx` — was **never applied**. Natalia researched the same terminals independently. So `apply_check.py` here is not auditing an apply process; it is diffing two independent research passes, and its class labels shift meaning accordingly.

184 staged records checked (184 landable, 0 re-verifications):

| Class | Count | What it actually means here |
|---|---|---|
| applied | 58 | Natalia independently reached the same value |
| not_applied | 37 | draft value absent from the live DB |
| diverged | 89 | the two passes reached different values |

**SOP §6's "multiple diverged edits → flag an apply-process error" does not fire**, because nothing was applied. Breaking the 89 down:

- **73 are `[ref]` columns** — and most reflect Natalia's refs being *richer* than the draft's, typically a merged multi-URL cell where the draft had one. That is the ref-merge discipline working, and it is why "diverged" is the majority class.
- **4 are `ResearcherNotes*`/`Wiki`** free-text, where divergence is expected.
- **12 are real value disagreements** — and all 12 went into the §3 spot-check sample, which is where their resolutions are.

Two things in the ref divergences do deserve attention:

**Ref replacement rather than merge.** Croatia · Krk `G100002043000` `Capacity [ref]`: the GIIGNL 2026 citation was replaced outright by a euractiv link rather than merged alongside it. Under the merge-never-replace rule a still-valid citation should have been carried forward. Worth a look across Natalia's ref edits with `audit_ref_drops.py` — I am reporting one instance, not a measured rate.

**Three filenames circulate for the GIIGNL 2026 edition**, and the diff surfaces two of them sitting in different cells:

| Where | Filename |
|---|---|
| repo archive | `GIIGNL-2026-Annual-Report-0526b.pdf` |
| the draft's citations | `…/1c9fdde3…/GIIGNL-2026-Annual-Report-final.pdf` |
| Natalia's citations | `…/ee1bebf9…/GIIGNL-2026-Annual-Report-0521.pdf` |

All three are **mirrors of one document = ONE source**, never two, so this is not a corroboration problem. `data/README.md` names the `ee1bebf9…-0521.pdf` elfsightcdn URL as canonical — which is the one Natalia used, so the live DB is already on the right URL and the draft's `-final.pdf` variant should be normalized to it wherever it would otherwise be staged.

## §6 Scaffolding fixed during this run

Both bugs were generating findings that did not exist. Both fixes are committed repo improvements.

**`citation_qc.py` — 5xx was being graded as rot.** An origin/edge error means the server refused or failed, not that the path is gone; CDN bot-walls routinely answer a scripted UA with 502/503 while serving a browser 200. I retested two offshore-energy.biz URLs with a browser User-Agent: both HTTP 200. **26 citations** were affected. Also added an `unverifiable` verdict so image-only PDFs stop counting as dead (**17** citations).

The effect on this memo's own headline is the reason to record it: the first pass read **158/1,262 = 12.5% dead with Montenegro at 63.2% and Albania at 34.2%, both over the §6 threshold**. After honest grading it is **117/1,355 = 8.6% with no country over threshold** — Montenegro 63.2% → **0%**, Greece 5.2% → 0%. Two countries would have been sent for exhaustive citation rebuilds they did not need. Re-grading was done offline from the stored `reason` fields, so no re-fetch of 1,262 URLs was required. `docs/sops/qc.md` §3.2 now documents the rule and the instruction to check what "dead" verdicts actually *are* before reporting any country over threshold.

**`completeness_sweep.py` — `orphan_ref` was 36, of which 33 were phantoms.** `StartDate [ref]` is the shared ref for the whole start-date family, but `REF_DATA_ALIASES` omitted `LatestPlannedStartYear`/`OriginalPlannedStartYear`. Every proposed/construction unit legitimately citing a *planned* start with no actual year yet was therefore reported as an orphan — 33 rows in this scope alone (verified against the export on Rostock, Mukran, Brunsbüttel, all of which have `LatestPlannedStartYear` populated). Rule F is about a ref with no paired value *anywhere in its family*, not one whose family member happens to be the planned rather than the actual year. **orphan_ref: 36 → 3.**
